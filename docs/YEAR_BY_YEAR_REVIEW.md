# Year-by-year West Bank review: Israeli military conduct, settler violence, expansion and displacement

**Coverage:** 2005–2025, occupied West Bank including East Jerusalem where the OCHA indicator covers it. Gaza and Lebanon require separate territorial series and are not combined here. This is an evidence-led review of documented incidents and trends; it does not itself determine whether a war crime occurred. “Israeli forces” and “Israeli settlers” are separated where the source does so. Figures for Palestinian casualties below are those OCHA attributes to Israeli forces or settlers; settler incident counts are a distinct measure and must not be added to casualty counts.

## Historical coverage

This West Bank statistical series begins in 2005. The historical, incident-level scan starts in 1948; see [Historical review: 1948 onward](HISTORICAL_REVIEW_1948_ONWARD.md) and the [coverage ledger](../data/historical_year_index.csv). Years not yet researched remain marked pending.

## Displacement scope and cautions

The annual OCHA chart is a consistent core series spanning 2009 onward, but its 2025 figure is not an all-context total. OCHA’s February 2026 snapshot reports 37,135 people displaced in 2025 across a broader set of categories, including 33,362 people displaced from northern West Bank camps in operations by Israeli or Palestinian forces. Because that camp count combines actors, it is not entered as an Israel-attributed total. OCHA separately identifies 20,805 displaced from Tulkarm and Nur Shams due to Israeli forces’ operations; that value is recorded as a separate contextual indicator and is not added to the core series pending overlap and category checks. The remaining Jenin camp figure (12,557) has mixed Israeli/Palestinian-force attribution and is excluded from the Israeli-only count.

The casualties line is attributed to Israeli forces **or** settlers in the comparable OCHA release; it does not disaggregate every year's fatalities and injuries by perpetrator. The settler incident count is separately reported, but it is not a count of Israeli military operations, raids, airstrikes or attacks. This version therefore does not claim to provide a year-by-year military-attack count. Where OCHA publishes explicit actor-specific breakdowns, they are preserved: for 2025 it reports 225 killed by Israeli forces, nine by settlers and six with attribution unresolved. It separately documents 20,805 people displaced from Tulkarm and Nur Shams camps due to Israeli forces' operations. Earlier military campaigns and operation-level records need a dedicated incident register before they can be compared as annual totals.

The annual settler figures are contextual indicators of violence by Israeli settlers. Inclusion of an individual settler incident in the primary case dataset still requires incident-level sourcing and application of the repository's attribution rules; the aggregate alone does not establish state participation or protection in each case.

“Displaced” means OCHA recorded people displaced in the specified context and reporting period. It is not the same as all people who experienced temporary evacuation, movement restrictions, home loss, or repeated displacement. Gaza displacement must be compiled separately, with explicit handling of repeated displacement and changing population denominators.

## Settlement expansion: compare like with like

- **2023:** Peace Now counted 12,349 housing units in West Bank plans advanced, excluding East Jerusalem; its narrative reports at least 26 newly established outposts. A planning-stage unit is not a completed unit.
- **2024:** The EU report records 9,884 units advanced in the West Bank and 18,988 in East Jerusalem, plus 24,258 dunams declared “State Land.” The geography and planning-stage definitions differ from Peace Now’s 2023 figure, so these should not be read as a direct year-to-year decline.
- **2025:** Peace Now reports 27,941 housing units advanced, 9,629 units included in tenders, 86 new outposts and 54 official settlements approved. Those measures describe different stages of policy and construction and should remain separate columns in any future expansion dataset.

These sources describe Israeli government plans, authorizations, land designations, infrastructure and outpost establishment. “Expansionism” here is the project’s analytical framing; each underlying event is recorded as a source-specific act, not as a legal finding. The International Court of Justice’s 2024 advisory opinion is summarized in the EU report; legal analysis should cite the Court itself in a dedicated legal section.
