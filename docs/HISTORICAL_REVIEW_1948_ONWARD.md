# Historical review: 1948 onward

**Project goal:** Scan year by year from 1948 forward for non-Israeli reporting and records documenting Israeli military attacks, bombings, killings, capture and detention of Palestinians, torture/abuse, forced displacement, and related expansionist policies. This is a continuing source review, not a completed timeline. The current evidence files are the [1948 event register](../data/historical_incidents.csv), [annual West Bank indicators, 2005–2025](../data/west_bank_annual_indicators.csv), and the separate [West Bank year-by-year review](YEAR_BY_YEAR_REVIEW.md).

## Evidence and attribution rules

- “War crime” is a research question and legal category, not a label applied just because an event is violent or a source uses that term. Record allegations, investigative findings, judicial findings, and disputed facts separately.
- Prefer contemporary non-Israeli news reporting, international observers, UN records, ICRC documents, and independent research. Preserve what a source could and could not know at the time.
- The state of Israel was proclaimed on 14 May 1948. Events before state establishment by Irgun, Lehi, or Haganah are not silently recoded as Israeli state acts. For the transition period after 14 May, record the force named by the source and mark chain-of-command uncertainty.
- The 1949 Fourth Geneva Convention was adopted after the 1948 events. Do not retroactively cite it as the governing treaty for a 1948 act; legal analysis must identify the law applicable at the time and distinguish later norms.
- Do not use the 1948 overall Palestinian refugee total as a count attributable exclusively to Israeli state actions. Use event-specific displacement where the actor, period, and source support attribution.
- Aggregate data is not an incident record. The 2005–2025 OCHA series begins later and does not fill the year-by-year historical record for 1948–2004.

## 1948: first pass

The 1948 record spans the Mandate's end, state formation, and the first Arab-Israeli war. This section focuses on episodes after Israel's declaration where Israeli state-force attribution is documented or explicitly provisional. It does not include Deir Yassin (9 April) as an Israeli state act: it preceded Israel's establishment and was attributed to Irgun and Lehi, not the Israeli state.

### Lydda and Ramle: capture, detention, expulsion (11–13 July)

A contemporary Pittsburgh Post-Gazette dispatch, filed from Lydda by Kenneth Bilby for the New York Herald Tribune, reported that the Israeli army had captured Lydda and Ramle and held thousands of Arab men: 3,000–4,000 at Lydda's central mosque and about 1,500 in wire enclosures at Ramle. The dispatch is valuable contemporaneous non-Israeli reporting on capture and detention, but it does not itself establish the later full expulsion history. Later historical research links the expulsions to Operation Dani and estimates 60,000–70,000 residents and refugees expelled. The [UN Mediator's 1948 account](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-198227/) separately recorded Israeli occupation of Lydda and Ramle and that most inhabitants fled, adding to the refugee population. Keep the contemporaneous report and later reconstruction as distinct evidence layers. Sources: [newspaper clipping](https://coveringpalestine.org/archive/cp-1948-109), [Journal of Palestine Studies on the detention camps](https://www.plands.org/getattachment/d3c2f9ce-20e1-4459-9dea-3d2c223d2396/the-icrc-and-the-detention-of-palestinian-civilians), [New Yorker reconstruction](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2013/10/21/lydda-1948).

### Tantura: alleged executions after capture (22–23 May)

Later non-Israeli reporting and research describe allegations that Alexandroni Brigade soldiers executed Palestinian men after the village's capture and surrender. The Guardian reported witness estimates ranging from 40 to 200; that range is not a verified death count. Forensic Architecture's [2023 investigation](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/executions-and-mass-graves-in-tantura-23-may-1948) combined testimony, photographs, maps, and aerial imagery to identify possible execution and mass-grave sites. The proposed grave locations have not been excavated, and the event remains disputed. A New York Times report republished by Bangladesh's bdnews24 records survivor accounts, Israeli veteran testimony, denial, and disagreements over the number and circumstances of deaths. Record the allegation and evidence; do not state an exact toll as established. Sources: [Forensic Architecture](https://forensic-architecture.org/investigation/executions-and-mass-graves-in-tantura-23-may-1948), [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2023/may/25/study-1948-israeli-massacre-tantura-palestinian-village-mass-graves-car-park), [NYT report republished by bdnews24](https://bdnews24.com/world/middle-east/old-palestinian-wound-resurfaces-at-israeli-resort).

### Ijzim, Ein Ghazal and Jaba: truce-period attacks and destruction (18–25 July)

The UN Mediator's [cable dated 12 September 1948](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/471054/files/S_999-EN.pdf) reports an investigation by UN observers. It found that the three villages were attacked by air and land, residents were forced to evacuate, and Ein Ghazal and Jaba were systematically destroyed after evacuation. The Mediator told Israel's Provisional Government the action was unjustified and the destruction excessive, violating the spirit and letter of the truce. The same source says Arab figures for the dead and captured were greatly exaggerated and reports fewer than 130 killed or missing; a UN Yearbook summary says observers located more than 8,000 people, while the Mediator cable says more than 4,000. Preserve this source-version discrepancy. These are UN findings about truce violations, not a criminal court judgment.

### Gaza bombing reported by Associated Press (16–19 October)

A contemporaneous Associated Press dispatch republished by the Havre Daily News on 19 October says Israeli aircraft had bombed Gaza for a fourth consecutive day and that Palestinian Arab civilians were reported fleeing. The clipping says the extent of flight could not be determined and gives no casualty count. This supports a reported bombing and flight account; it does not establish displacement totals, targeting, or a war crime. [Archived clipping](https://coveringpalestine.org/archive/cp-1948-045).

### Civilian internment, POW/labor camps, and abuse (May–November)

The ICRC visited Arab prisoners in Israel during 1948. Its [historical summary](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/150-years-humanitarian-action-protecting-prisoners-and-detainees) says it visited approximately 2,000 Arab POWs in Israel in June; that category is not limited to Palestinian civilians. Abu Sitta and Rempel's [Journal of Palestine Studies research](https://www.plands.org/getattachment/d3c2f9ce-20e1-4459-9dea-3d2c223d2396/the-icrc-and-the-detention-of-palestinian-civilians) analyzes ICRC archival reports and former prisoners' testimonies. It estimates around 5,000 Palestinian civilian internees across four official camps, reports their use as forced labor, and documents testimony of beatings, torture, deprivation, and deaths. The article also cites ICRC delegates' observations of wounds from abuse and beatings. Distinguish testimony from direct observation, and do not treat combined POW totals as a precise civilian-internment count.

### Operation Hiram and Galilee roundups (late October–November)

The same study, citing a 16 November UN observer report and survivor accounts, estimates that about three-quarters of approximately 60,000 Palestinian Arab residents of the area were displaced during Operation Hiram and reports hundreds of men and boys rounded up and sent to camps. It reports roughly 200 men detained from al-Bi'na. Those figures are estimates attributed to the study and its sources, not a complete census. The event register preserves actor, scope, source, and count limitations separately.

### Beersheba and al-Dawayima (late October)

The detention study cites Ben-Gurion's diary for approximately 100 men held captive after Beersheba's capture, put to work, and taken to a prisoners' camp, alongside the town's population being sent to Gaza. This needs cross-checking against the underlying diary and displacement records.

For al-Dawayima, the current record contains an Arab Refugee Congress allegation preserved in a 1949 UN Conciliation Commission working paper and the Israeli government's November 1948 denial, which says civilians had left before the town was occupied. The available sources reviewed here do not independently settle the massacre allegation. It stays in the lead register and contributes no confirmed fatality count.

## 1949 initial scan

### Gaza aerial and artillery attacks (2–7 January)

Abu Sitta and Rempel's peer-reviewed study reports six separate Israeli aerial and artillery attacks on Gaza city and the refugee camps at Khan Yunis, Bureij, Rafah, and Deir al-Balah, citing ICRC field reports. It gives a combined report of about 190 killed and more than 400 wounded across six days. The excerpt does not disaggregate civilian and combatant casualties or give an incident-by-incident table. The ICRC delegation head is quoted describing the attacks as lacking military objectives; treat this as the delegate's assessment in the archival source, not a court finding. Obtain the original ICRC report before treating the number as final. [Journal of Palestine Studies article](https://www.plands.org/getattachment/4b5dcb82-271b-47a4-91d5-472154638b0e/the-icrc-and-the-detention-of-palestinian-civilians).

### Detention and continuing displacement

The same study cites UN truce observers for the January capture of two named villagers near Tubas while they worked their land, and quotes ICRC mission head Jacques de Reynier's April 1949 report that Israeli authorities continued arresting able-bodied men and placing them in camps without due process. It also describes Operation Megrafa, running December 1948–July 1949, as an effort to prevent refugees from returning; specific cases and totals require separate source review. Do not infer a year total from this pattern. [Article and cited archival leads](https://www.plands.org/getattachment/4b5dcb82-271b-47a4-91d5-472154638b0e/the-icrc-and-the-detention-of-palestinian-civilians).

A short U.S. newspaper report dated 8 April relays an expelled Rameh resident's account that 41 men, including 28 Christians, were deported by Israeli authorities. This is a single-witness report awaiting independent corroboration. [Archived clipping](https://coveringpalestine.org/clippings/1940s).

### Settlement and repopulation on Palestinian land

An 18 January New York Times report by Anne O'Hare McCormick describes the Israeli government clearing a wrecked Arab village for settlement, seven new settlements started in Galilee on land left by Palestinians, and plans for 51 more. This is contemporary reporting of state settlement activity and its effect on return prospects. The reported numbers are starts/plans, not verified completions or parcel-level legal findings. The article's estimate of 750,000 refugees is a broad conflict-wide stock estimate and is not used as an annual, Israel-attributed displacement total. [Archived NYT clipping](https://coveringpalestine.org/clippings/1940s).

A 30 January AP field report describes refugee overcrowding in Egyptian-held southern Palestine. It quotes Egyptian Army representatives estimating 225,000 refugees behind their lines, including 70,000 in Gaza and 95,000 in Khan Yunis. These are attributed population-stock estimates and humanitarian context, not a count of people newly displaced by Israel in 1949. [Archived AP clipping](https://coveringpalestine.org/clippings/1940s).

## 1950 initial scan

### El Auja Bedouin roundup and expulsion (August–September)

The UNTSO Chief of Staff's 18 September report transmits an investigation by the Egyptian-Israeli Mixed Armistice Commission. It says Israeli forces rounded up about 4,000 Bedouins around the El Auja demilitarized zone and drove them across the Egyptian border by 2 September. Representatives of five tribes told the investigators that Israeli operations to clear the area began 20 August, used troops, armored cars, and reconnaissance aircraft, and that Israeli forces burned tents, crops, and belongings after the expulsion. They reported 13 Bedouins killed. Israel denied entering the demilitarized zone, said the El Auja group followed Bedouins expelled from outside it, and characterized many as infiltrators. Preserve the reported deaths as witness statements conveyed through the commission, and do not convert the entire account into a judicial finding. The same report notes approximately 1,000 other Arab expulsions since March; that cumulative figure may overlap with other cases and is not added to 4,000. [UN report S/1797](https://digitallibrary.un.org/nanna/record/605772/files/S_SUPP_1950_3--%5EOR_SC_1950_3%5E-EN.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0).

### Majdal residents transferred to Gaza (summer–October)

The Jewish Telegraphic Agency reported on 16 October that the last 75 Arab families from Migdal Gad/Majdal had been transferred to Gaza the previous day. A later UN Mixed Armistice Commission report records about 2,000 Arabs expelled from El-Majdal by Israeli authorities and calls for repatriation and restoration of rights/property for those found entitled to return. Use the 2,000 as the commission's estimate, and 75 families as the reported final group, not a separate total. The contemporary report's language does not establish whether consent was free; the UN commission's later description and remedy should be retained alongside Israel's explanation in the underlying proceedings. [JTA report](https://www.jta.org/archive/israelis-debanese-study-boundary-demarcation-75-arab-families-repatriated-to-gaza); [UN commission report](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/479809/files/S_2388-EN.pdf).

## 1951 initial scan

### Wadi Ara village expulsions and land seizure (February)

A contemporary Palestinian press report provides a lead on continued displacement inside the new state: Adel Manna's University of California Press monograph cites *Al-Ittihad* of 10 February 1951 reporting that inhabitants of thirteen small Wadi Ara villages were uprooted and their lands confiscated. A later UN Special Committee report also records the 13-village expulsion and land confiscation. The original Arabic newspaper item and village-by-village population, destination, and orders still need review; the register therefore records no displaced-person total. [Manna, *Nakba and Survival*](https://webfiles.ucpress.edu/oa/9780520389373_WEB.pdf); [UN report A/39/360](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/66827/files/A_39_360-EN.pdf).

### Border village attacks and reported civilian deaths (25 January–13 February)

The UNTSO Chief of Staff's report S/2048 lists Jordanian complaints that Israeli forces attacked border villages during this period. The entries include claims of two civilians killed in Saffa, ten killed and eight wounded in Sharafat after two houses were blown up, and one man and two children killed in Falam, alongside attacks or other actions reported at Beit Iksa, Beit Surik, Yalu, Deir Ballut, and Imwas. On 14 February the Mixed Armistice Commission condemned wanton killings and home destruction collectively and treated the complaints as acted upon; the report does not separately adjudicate each allegation or give independent casualty verification for every event. These figures remain complaint-level reports, not confirmed annual totals. [UNTSO report S/2048](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/479492/files/S_2048-EN.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0).

### Shelling of Idna (7 March)

The Jordan-Israel Mixed Armistice Commission found that shelling of Idna by Israeli forces constituted a technical violation of the General Armistice Agreement and condemned it. The decision also said the violation resulted from lesser contraventions by Jordanian residents and ordered the demarcation line to be marked clearly. The quoted finding gives no casualty number and is an armistice decision, not a criminal judgment. [UN report of the Mixed Armistice Commissions](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/605779/files/S_SUPP_1951_4--%5EOR_SC_1951_4%5E-EN.pdf).

## 1952 initial scan

### Beit Jala-area armed raid (6–7 January)

The UNTSO Chief of Staff reported Jordan's account that a group of armed Israelis entered Jordan-controlled territory and blew up houses on the outskirts of Beit Jala, killing six people and seriously wounding three. Leaflets linked the attack to an unsolved killing in Israel. The Mixed Armistice Commission unanimously found a serious breach of Article III(2), expressed horror, and called on Israel's delegation to prevent recurrence. The report does not identify the attackers as an IDF unit or establish their chain of command. A Palestinian historical archive repeats the UN tally and places the incident in its history of violence against Palestinians; it is not independent casualty verification. [UNTSO report S/2833](https://unispal.un.org/pdfs/S2833.pdf); [Our Palestine retrospective](https://pal48.ps/en/article/72/bethlehem-massacre).

### Bedouin camp raid near El Berig (6 January)

A separate Egyptian-Israeli Mixed Armistice Commission file documents an armed raid on a Bedouin camp near the Gaza demarcation line, close to El Berig. A UN military observer who visited on 7 January recorded seven deaths (three men, one woman, two boys and one girl), twelve wounded, eight tents destroyed or damaged, and a destroyed water pump and pump house. The initial Egyptian complaint gave a different wounded tally and described one woman dying later; the observer also recorded gunfire striking a nearby tuberculosis hospital. The observer attributed the crossing to armed Israelis, but did not identify a military unit or command order. No displacement count is supplied. [UN document S/3186](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/602634/files/S_3186-EN.pdf).

### Killing of the Salem brothers (30 March)

The same UN file records an Egyptian complaint that armed Israelis detained a family working a field near the demarcation line, released the father and young daughter, and killed his two sons. A UN military observer investigated the same day and reported that six armed Israelis had crossed the line; tracks, bloodspots and spent cartridges supported the account. The observer described the brothers as shot at close range and noted bayonet wounds. The report identifies no unit or court finding. [UN document S/3186](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/602634/files/S_3186-EN.pdf).

### Killing of Hussein Gaber Ibrahim (12 April; attribution unresolved)

An Egyptian complaint alleged that armed Israelis killed Ibrahim while he worked his field. A UN observer documented the body and scene and found Israeli-marked cartridge cases, but stated there were no eyewitnesses. Record this as an attribution lead rather than a confirmed Israeli-perpetrator finding. [UN document S/3186](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/602634/files/S_3186-EN.pdf).

### Qaffin-area house bombing (May; Commission consideration 28 May)

The same report records a Jordanian complaint about a house blown up near Qaffin, with a woman and four children aged one to fifteen killed. The Commission considered it a breach of Article IV(3); the Jordanian delegation and chair voted in favour, while the Israeli delegate abstained. The report notes leaflets and penetration about five kilometres into Jordan-controlled territory but does not establish the perpetrators' military or state affiliation. The exact incident date is not supplied. [UNTSO report S/2833](https://unispal.un.org/pdfs/S2833.pdf).

### Es Sani Bedouin forced transfer and temporary displacement (September)

On 21 September, Jordan complained that approximately 800 Es Sani Bedouins had been expelled from the Beersheba area into Jordan-controlled territory. The UN investigation indicated that the group had crossed to escape a forced transfer to less desirable land elsewhere inside Israel-controlled territory. A negotiated arrangement returned those who crossed to Israel. This is a documented coercive-transfer episode affecting about 800 people, but the report does not establish a permanent expulsion of all 800 or an annual net displacement total. It does not name the individual officials who ordered the proposed transfer. [UNTSO report S/2833](https://unispal.un.org/pdfs/S2833.pdf).

### Wadi Ara / Kafr Ara shooting (June; date disputed)

Later archival studies report that Israeli soldiers shot civilians gathering near the armistice line to meet relatives across the border during Eid al-Fitr; two people were killed and five wounded. Adel Manna dates the shooting to 23 June and describes the visits as customary and permitted, with an internal committee later treating the deaths as an unpremeditated error. Zaki Shalom dates it to 25 June and records the IDF account alongside civilian and political objections. Until the cited committee minutes and contemporary press are checked, retain 23–25 June as a date conflict. The casualty count is documented in retrospective scholarship, not a non-Israeli contemporary news report. [Manna, *Nakba and Survival*](https://webfiles.ucpress.edu/oa/9780520389373_WEB.pdf); [Shalom, INSS](https://www.inss.org.il/strategic_assessment/the-debate-on-the-idf-open-fire-orders-1952/).

No accessible non-Israeli contemporary newspaper report was located in this tranche. The UN armistice report is the strongest contemporaneous source for the first three cases. The only defensible 1952 displacement figure found so far is approximately 800 people affected by the Es Sani forced-transfer episode; it is not a total of people permanently displaced that year.

## 1953 initial scan

### Falameh and Rantis attacks (22/23 and 28–29 January)

UNTSO and the Mixed Armistice Commission recorded Israeli military attacks across the demarcation line at Falameh and Rantis. The UK Foreign Secretary told Parliament that the Commission majority held Israel responsible for armistice breaches. With casualty information still incomplete, he reported at least three Arabs killed and 10 or 12 wounded; the questioner's claim of at least ten deaths was not confirmed in the minister's answer. Keep that provisional count distinct from more detailed village-level totals pending review of the underlying MAC investigation files. [UN Security Council meeting 630](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/631616/files/S_PV-630-EN.pdf); [UK Hansard, 9 February 1953](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1953-02-09/debates/dc9614af-5cfd-42f3-9e49-8b2d9b95e0de/JordanianVillages(IsraeliAttack)).

### Bureij refugee-camp raid (28–29 August)

UNRWA's acting director reported 20 refugees killed, 27 seriously wounded and 35 less serious casualties during an armed raid on Bureij. The report said more than 1,800 refugees fled to other camps after the attack. UNTSO reported that the Mixed Armistice Commission majority attributed the raid to armed Israelis; it described a reprisal motive as likely, not established. Treat the 1,800-plus as temporary flight after the attack, not a permanent displacement or an annual net total. [UNTSO record, Security Council meeting 630](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/631616/files/S_PV-630-EN.pdf); [UNRWA report preserved in UN document S/3186](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/602634/files/S_3186-EN.pdf).

### Qibya raid (14–15 October)

UNTSO reported that roughly half a battalion of Israel's regular army crossed into Qibya; part of the force entered Shuqba, while supporting forces shelled Budrus. The UNTSO investigation recorded 42 killed, 15 wounded, and 41 houses plus a school destroyed at Qibya. Jordan's 16 October letter reported 42 bodies recovered and additional possible victims beneath wreckage. On 24 November, Security Council Resolution 101 found the Qibya retaliatory action violated the cease-fire and was inconsistent with the Armistice Agreement and Charter obligations. The resolution is an institutional finding, not a criminal judgment. The UNTSO report did not identify who committed the alleged attack in Israel that preceded the raid. No defensible displaced-person total appears in the reviewed records. [UNTSO report to the Security Council](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/631616/files/S_PV-630-EN.pdf); [Security Council Resolution 101](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/280556?ln=en).

This is an initial scan, not an exhaustive annual record. Contemporary non-Israeli newspaper archives and additional incident-level records remain to be checked; the UK Hansard record is a contemporaneous parliamentary source, not a newspaper.

## 1954 initial scan

### Nahalin night raid (28–29 March)

Jordan reported that armed Israelis attacked Nahalin near Bethlehem shortly after midnight. UN observers examined damaged homes and the mosque, interviewed wounded people and witnesses, and followed footprints from the village toward the demarcation line and matching tracks on the Israeli side. The Mixed Armistice Commission adopted a resolution condemning Israel; its chair said the evidence established guilt without question. The UNTSO report records nine killed: five National Guards, one woman in the village, and three Arab Legionnaires killed when their reinforcement vehicle was destroyed. It records fourteen villagers wounded, plus an officer and four Legionnaires wounded. Jordan's initial complaint reported eighteen civilian wounded; a same-day JTA report gave preliminary figures of ten killed and sixteen wounded. Preserve those as separate early counts. No displacement figure was found in the reviewed records. [UNTSO report S/3251](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/603129/files/S_3251-EN.pdf); [Jordan's complaint S/3192](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/602666/files/S_3192-EN.pdf); [JTA, 30 March 1954](https://www.jta.org/archive/jordan-village-reported-attacked-by-israelis-ten-arabs-killed); [Our Palestine retrospective](https://pal48.ps/en/article/60/nahalin-massacre-1954-a-night-raid-on-a-west-bank-village).

This is an initial incident scan, not a complete annual review. Additional contemporary press and records, settlement/land expansion, detention and displacement totals remain to be checked.

## 1955 initial scan

### Gaza military raid / Operation Black Arrow (28 February–1 March)

The UNTSO investigation found that Israeli regular forces crossed the demarcation line east of Gaza and attacked an Egyptian military camp, as well as the stationmaster's house and a water-pump house serving Gaza. The Mixed Armistice Commission found the operation prearranged, planned, and ordered by Israeli authorities. Its figures separated 36 Egyptian military deaths from two civilian deaths and 29 military wounded from two civilian wounded; Egypt's initial complaint gave slightly higher military figures. One civilian killed was a seven-year-old Palestinian boy. The Security Council condemned the attack as inconsistent with the cease-fire, Armistice Agreement, and Charter obligations. No displacement count was found. [UNTSO report S/3373](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/572533/files/S_3373-EN.pdf); [Security Council decision via UN Yearbook](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/860243/files/1122956-EN.pdf?ln=ru).

### Shooting and removal of Hassan Abu Anza (9 March)

The Mixed Armistice Commission found that an Israeli patrol fired across the demarcation line at unarmed Palestinian farmer Hassan Abu Anza while he planted watermelons in the Abbassan area. He was wounded; two armed Israelis crossed, removed him and his donkey to Israeli-controlled territory. The Commission found a flagrant armistice violation and requested his return. The record reviewed here does not establish the later outcome. [UNTSO/MAC decision](https://unispal.un.org/pdfs/2853648FAF27622D052564F800568102.pdf).

### Shelling of Gaza and Deir el Balla (5 April)

A U.S. diplomatic telegram relaying UNTSO information records Israeli artillery and mortar impacts in Deir el Balla and Gaza town. Initial Egyptian casualty figures in the 5 April cable were explicitly unconfirmed; a 6 April update relayed the latest figures as 55 civilians killed and 102 wounded, with an earlier account noting 17 children among the dead. These are evolving figures reported through a diplomat, not a final casualty determination. No displacement count was found. [FRUS telegram on the 5 April shelling](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v15/d247).

### Khan Yunis and Bani Suheila raid (31 August–1 September)

UNTSO reported that an Israeli light-armoured unit entered Egyptian-controlled territory, machine-gunned and demolished the Khan Yunis police station, partly destroyed a hospital under construction, machine-gunned buildings and homes in Bani Suheila, and attacked an Egyptian position. The UNTSO investigation was incomplete: it relayed Egyptian authorities' report of 36 killed and 13 wounded among police, armed forces and civilians, but could not establish the exact number, and observers had seen few of the listed casualties. A contemporaneous on-scene New York Times report, republished by Covering Palestine, counted 35 dead in its text, including eight villagers, 15 policemen, and 12 Palestinian Arab soldiers; its headline said 39. Keep these source counts separate; no reliable Palestinian civilian-only total or displacement count is established. [UNTSO report S/3430](https://digitallibrary.un.org/nanna/record/605332/files/S_SUPP_1955_3--%5EOR_SC_1955_3%5E-EN.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0); [contemporary on-scene report](https://coveringpalestine.org/archive/cp-1955-015).

This is an initial scan. No defensible annual displaced-person count was found for 1955. Gaza held a large pre-existing refugee population; that population stock is not a measure of new displacement during this year. A systematic review of 1955 detention, land/settlement expansion, and additional foreign press remains open.

## 1956 initial scan

### Israeli occupation and administration of Gaza (November 1956–March 1957)

Israel's forces occupied the Gaza Strip beginning 1 November. UNRWA reported curfews imposed by the military authorities prevented the usual ration distribution; daily curfew breaks allowed deliveries to resume and rations reached each registered refugee by 15 November. The Agency said the occupation caused civilian casualties and fear among both refugees and local residents, and that UNTSO could not investigate because Israeli authorities restricted observer movement. UNRWA documented mass screening operations and large numbers taken to interrogation centres, but supplied no detainee total. It reported that more than 213,000 ration-recipient refugees lived in Gaza; this is a population stock, not people newly displaced in 1956. No reliable annual displacement-flow count was found. A UN representative later said administrative measures appeared to facilitate permanence of the military-created situation. Nur Masalha's archival study documents Israeli proposals during the occupation to resettle Gaza's Palestinian refugees elsewhere; these were proposals, not evidence of completed transfer. [UNRWA special report A/3212/Add.1](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/710955/files/A_3212_Add-1-EN.pdf); [UN Yearbook 1956](https://digitallibrary.un.org/nanna/record/860242/files/1122955-EN.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0); [Masalha study](https://fada.birzeit.edu/bitstream/20.500.11889/8300/1/The%201956-57%20occupation%20of%20the%20Gaza%20Strip%20Israeli%20proposals%20to%20resettle%20the%20Palestinian%20refugees.pdf).

### Khan Yunis killings during occupation (3 November)

UNRWA reported that Israeli troops occupied Khan Yunis and its adjacent refugee camp. Accounts of resistance conflicted: Israeli authorities said refugees participated in resistance; refugees said fighting had ceased and troops killed unarmed civilians while searching for men with weapons. The Director received lists from sources he considered trustworthy naming 275 people allegedly killed: 140 refugees and 135 local residents. He cautioned that the exact number of dead and wounded was not known. Treat 275 as a reported names-list, not a fully verified death toll. [UNRWA special report, paragraph 23](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/710955/files/A_3212_Add-1-EN.pdf).

### Rafah refugee-camp shooting during screening (12 November)

UNRWA reported that both Israeli authorities and its own sources agreed refugees were killed and wounded by occupying forces during a screening operation, though the causes and numbers were disputed. Israel said refugees were hostile and resisted; refugees denied resistance. UNRWA said the camp's loudspeaker announcement had not reached some sections, insufficient time was allowed, and some Israeli soldiers apparently panicked and fired at a running crowd. The Agency received lists naming 111 people allegedly killed: 103 refugees, seven local residents and one Egyptian. It could not verify each death or complete the list of all dead and missing. Keep the list size distinct from an independently confirmed toll. UNRWA separately listed 66 additional people allegedly killed from 1–21 November, including 48 refugees; individual deaths were unverified and should not be added to the Khan Yunis/Rafah lists. [UNRWA special report, paragraphs 24–30](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/710955/files/A_3212_Add-1-EN.pdf).

### Kafr Qasim curfew killings (29 October)

Israeli Border Police shot Palestinian citizens returning from work after a curfew was moved to 17:00 with very short notice. A contemporary Associated Press dispatch published when Israel disclosed the incident on 12 December said men, women and children had been killed but gave no total. Contemporary JTA reporting on the 1958 military tribunal said eight Border Police were convicted of murder in 43 deaths and that the court called Colonel Issachar Shadmi's order manifestly illegal; it said Major Shmuel Malinki should have refused it. A 1959 JTA report used the broader count of 49 deaths and reported that Shadmi was fined for exceeding his authority but acquitted of murder. Keep 43 court-conviction deaths and the later 49-person toll separate pending review of the full judgment and death records. [AP report at official disclosure](https://coveringpalestine.org/archive/cp-1956-012); [JTA on the tribunal ruling](https://www.jta.org/archive/military-tribunal-terms-shooting-order-in-kfar-kassem-illegal); [JTA on Shadmi's separate verdict](https://www.jta.org/archive/military-court-acquits-commander-in-kafr-kassem-killings).

### Husan, Wadi Fukin, Sharafa and Khader raids (25–26 September)

The Mixed Armistice Commission majority found that Israeli regular forces carried out a major, unprovoked and premeditated attack on Jordanian territory, using artillery, armored vehicles and aircraft. Its detailed resolution records 12 National Guards killed near Husan and two civilians wounded; it also records a demolished school at Wadi Fukin, 25 Jordanians killed and six wounded at the Sharafa police post, and a 12-year-old girl killed and three civilians wounded at Khader. Jordan's complaint reported 25 killed and six wounded across the area, while a later British parliamentary account cited approximately 39 deaths. These are different source scopes and cannot be summed. The military and police deaths are not counted as Palestinian civilian casualties. [UNTSO/MAC report S/3670](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/634563/files/S_3670-EN.pdf); [Jordan complaint S/3678](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/634571/files/S_3678-EN.pdf); [UK Hansard, 5 December 1956](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1956-12-05/debates/e5b72fa5-ed96-49cc-b8fd-659825250f87/MiddleEast).

### Qalqiliya raid (10–11 October)

UN military observers investigated the Israeli army's attack on the Qalqiliya police post and town. They documented the police post and adjacent pumping house demolished with explosives, shell impacts and damage to houses and the school. They counted 48 Jordanian dead: 43 army, National Guard and police, three civilians and two unidentified. Israel reported 18 of its forces killed. The 48 is a mixed total, not a civilian count; the Mixed Armistice Commission meeting on Jordan's complaint did not reach a completed finding. A contemporary JTA dispatch reported the raid and UN casualty count. [UNTSO report S/3685](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/634584/files/S_3685-EN.pdf); [JTA report, 12 October 1956](https://www.jta.org/archive/israelis-destroy-jordanian-stronghold-in-seven-hour-battle).

This remains an initial scan. No defensible 1956 annual Palestinian displacement total was identified. Detainee totals, additional raids, and settlement/land policy research remain open. Refugee population totals and proposed resettlement plans are not counted as actual annual displacement.

## 1957 initial scan

### Israeli land preparation in the Government House neutral zone (21 July onward)

UNTSO reported that Israeli workmen began staking out part of the zone between the Israel-Jordan armistice demarcation lines on 21 July. They used earthmoving and agricultural equipment to cut roads and plough land for a stated afforestation and beautification project. UNTSO observed up to about fifteen armed Israeli Border Police protecting laborers; it did not observe Israeli military personnel in the zone except officers who inspected it twice. The report says Israel had progressively extended cultivation in the area since 1949 and that records held by the UN Conciliation Commission indicated the zone was predominantly Arab-owned. However, determining title to particular parcels required a registry search; UNTSO noted that ownership of 50–60 uprooted olive trees was not established. It could not authoritatively decide the competing civilian land-use claims. Jordan alleged armed positions and fencing; those allegations should remain attributed rather than folded into UNTSO's findings. UNTSO also documented Jordanian troops, military works and military traffic in the zone. It recommended suspending the Israeli afforestation project pending negotiations and respecting ownership established by land registries. Security Council Resolution 127 followed in January 1958, so it is a later decision, not a 1957 finding. No deaths, injuries, arrests or displacement were reported in the reviewed UN record. [UNTSO report S/3892](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/577500/files/S_3892-EN.pdf); [Dulles message to Ben-Gurion, 23 October 1957](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1955-57v17/d388); [Security Council Resolution 127 (1958)](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/280583).

### Gaza withdrawal and reported removal of 25 families (March)

A. J. Liebling's contemporaneous field report says Israel's four-month occupation of Gaza ended in March 1957 and that Israeli forces removed twenty-five families during withdrawal. The report does not identify the families, destination, whether they consented, or the legal basis; record this as a contemporary report of removal, not a confirmed forced expulsion or a complete displacement count. The article gives UNRWA's Gaza refugee population as 219,423 at its last count; that is a population stock, not 1957 displacement. [Liebling, The New Yorker, 9 March 1957](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1957/03/16/letter-from-gaza).

This remains an initial scan. The 25 families are a numerical displacement lead, not an annual flow total. No annual total for Palestinian displacement, detention or civilian casualties has been established. Broader non-Israeli press searches and additional incident, settlement, land-policy, detention and abuse research remain open.

### Reported deportation of al-Syajj Arabs in the Negev (1957)

A Palestinian researcher’s endnote states that Israel deported al-Syajj Arabs in the Negev in 1957, citing Hillel Cohen (2001) and Wakim Wakim (2001). The article gives no date, number of people, destination, or operation details. This is a research lead grounded in secondary Palestinian scholarship, not a verified person-level count; the cited works and underlying archival/community evidence remain to be checked. Do not use it to calculate 1957’s annual displacement flow. ## 1958 initial scan

### Issawiya access restrictions and police shootings (1–3 June)

UNTSO records worsening friction between Israeli police patrols and Issawiya residents. Its Chief of Staff reported that Israeli authorities closed the only reasonable road available to villagers on 1 June. On 2 June, a UN observer directly saw Israeli police fire during a confrontation over the road; one villager was killed. The report says villagers had not produced firearms during the scuffle and does not identify the person killed. A separate 3 June JTA dispatch relayed the Israeli spokesman’s account that police fired at a group advancing toward a roadblock and one person was wounded; Israeli claims that the group was armed and directed by Jordan were not independently verified in that report. Keep the two shootings separate and attribute the JTA details to the Israeli official account. [UNTSO report S/4030 and addendum S/4030/Add.1](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/630390/files/S_4030_Add.1-EN.pdf); [JTA, 3 June 1958](https://www.jta.org/archive/100-jordanians-seek-to-enter-israel-territory-on-mt-scopus-one-wounded).

The 26 May Mount Scopus firefight is related context, not a finding that Israel initiated an attack: UNTSO relied on observer evidence and competing Israeli/Jordanian investigations, and the initial firing attribution remained disputed. The July addendum’s ballistic test established that the bullet killing UNTSO officer Lt. Col. George Flint had been fired from Jordanian-controlled territory. Keep this mixed exchange out of Israeli-attributed casualty totals. [UNTSO report S/4030](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/605630).

### Tamra land confiscation for IDP resettlement (October)

Palestinian researcher Nihad Boqa’i reports that the Israeli government confiscated about 56 plots from Tamra in October 1958 to house internally displaced Palestinians, citing Hillel Cohen (2001) and Israel State Archives Housing Ministry file 4410/61621. The source does not identify plot owners, resettled families, compensation, or an affected-person count. The act is recorded as state land policy; it does not establish that Tamra residents were displaced or that 56 plots equal 56 people. Boqa’i says land was also taken in Judaida and al-Jish months later, but the timing is not precise enough to assign those events to 1958. [Boqa’i, *Palestinian Internally Displaced Persons inside Israel*](https://pij.org/articles/1218/palestinian-internally-displaced-persons-inside-israel-challenging-the-solid-structures).

This remains an initial scan. No defensible annual Palestinian displacement total for 1958 was identified. The Mount Scopus exchange, police incidents, Tamra land policy, additional detention and abuse cases, and non-Israeli press coverage need further review; do not turn refugee population stocks or land plot counts into displacement totals.

## 1959 initial scan

### Cross-border killings and airspace incidents (January–November)

The Egyptian-Israel Mixed Armistice Commission found Israeli jets crossed into UAR airspace and fought with intercepted aircraft on 8 January; the Security Council transcript quotes its condemnation. On 4 February, the Commission found an armed Israeli patrol crossed the frontier south of Rafah and killed Fatima Mohammed, 25, and four-year-old Silmi Ide, while wounding another woman. Its investigation cited physical tracks, blood, bodies and Hebrew-marked ammunition. On 17 February, the Commission found a trained Israeli group crossed about three kilometres into UAR territory and ambushed four people: two were killed and one wounded. In November, it again found Israeli jets crossed into UAR airspace over Sinai and engaged in air combat. These actions took place in UAR territory, outside Palestine proper, and are included because they are Israeli actions in the surrounding region. The Commission issued findings and condemnations, not criminal judgments; Israel had boycotted its proceedings since 1956. No casualty count was reported for the air incidents. [UN Security Council transcript S/PV.845](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/631449/files/S_PV-845-EN.pdf); [Commission decision S/4160](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/605638/files/S_SUPP_1959_1--%5EOR_SC_1959_1%5E-EN.pdf); [Commission decision S/4167](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/628097/files/S_4167-EN.pdf); [Commission decision S/4240 in the UN supplement](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/605640/files/S_SUPP_1959_4--%5EOR_SC_1959_4%5E-EN.pdf).

JTA reported a separate 17 July border clash using the Israeli Army spokesman’s account: two Egyptians killed and three wounded and captured, after alleged infiltrators opened fire. This is retained as a provisional lead, not independently confirmed fact; no independent investigation was located in this pass. JTA also reported that the Mixed Armistice Commission strongly blamed four Israeli fighters for attacking/intercepting a UAR commercial airliner over the Gulf of Aqaba on 13 September. The plane reached Cairo, no injuries were reported, and Israel denied firing. [JTA, 17 July 1959](https://www.jta.org/archive/two-egyptians-killed-three-wounded-in-clash-with-israel-border-patrol); [JTA, 25 September 1959](https://www.jta.org/archive/armistice-body-censures-israel-for-attack-on-egyptian-airliner).

### Azazme expulsions and displacement count (September–October)

The Mixed Armistice Commission found that Israeli troops expelled about 350 Azazme Bedouin from Israeli-controlled Negev territory across the international frontier into the UAR beginning 18 September and in following days. It found that troops killed some Bedouin, burned tents, deprived people of property, and compelled flight; it separately found that Israeli fire killed one Bedouin on UAR territory. The Commission described the treatment as harsh and cruel and found violations of armistice and Security Council provisions. Israel’s response disputed the Commission’s process and said the Bedouin crossed back into Sinai following an operation after an Israeli officer was killed; this remains Israel’s party account and does not erase the Commission’s finding. JTA separately reported three Bedouin killed during a patrol clash, but that number is not added to the Commission record until overlap is resolved. [Commission decision S/4226](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/628197/files/S_4226-EN.pdf); [Israel’s response S/4231](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/628215/files/S_4231-EN.pdf); [JTA, 21 September 1959](https://www.jta.org/archive/three-negev-beduin-killed-in-clash-with-israel-patrol-at-sinai-border).

In October, JTA reported a separate Jordanian complaint involving more than 100 Azazme Bedouin said to have been expelled into Jordan; a follow-up reported Israel offered to examine possible readmission if residence/citizenship could be shown. The identity and timing of this group, and whether it overlaps the September expulsions into the UAR, remain unresolved. Keep the figure separate and do not add it to 350. The 1959 annual displacement flow remains unknown; about 350 is a Commission estimate for one expulsion episode, not a complete annual total. [JTA, 21 October 1959](https://www.jta.org/archive/armistice-body-to-discuss-alleged-expulsion-of-beduins-by-israel); [JTA, 23 October 1959](https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-offers-re-admission-of-beduins-must-prove-israeli-residence).

### Negev state policy planning

Historical scholarship reports that Israel’s government decided in August 1959 to retain military administration over Bedouin and draft a settlement law. December committees considered land registration in the southern Negev and legislation concerning settlement in the eastern Negev. These are recorded as policy planning only; the reviewed source does not establish that the proposed law or relocation was implemented, identify affected households, or prove a land seizure. [Yahel and Kark, historical study](https://doi.org/10.1080/13530194.2017.1343122).

The initial 1959 scan found no documented Israeli action in Lebanon and no U.S. citizen or U.S. forces victim lead; this is a search result, not proof that none occurred. The annual displacement total is unknown. A separate UNTSO investigation of the January 23 shepherd killing on the Israel–Syria frontier left the initial-firing attribution disputed, so it is not included as an Israeli-attributed attack. [UNTSO report S/4164](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/605638/files/S_SUPP_1959_1--%5EOR_SC_1959_1%5E-EN.pdf).

## 1960 initial scan

### Khirbat at Tawafiq attack and Qunaytirah overflight (31 January–1 February)

UNTSO investigated a series of clashes in the southern Israel-Syria demilitarized zone. The Israel-Syrian Mixed Armistice Commission found that regular Israeli armed forces attacked Khirbat at Tawafiq overnight on 31 January–1 February. It said UN military observers found the village had only a protective trench and barbed wire, and that the attack caused near-total destruction, two deaths and two wounded. The Commission condemned the attack and found a flagrant violation of the armistice. JTA contemporaneously reported that about 50 houses had been leveled, while describing the village as a Syrian military base; Israel said the raid targeted an illegal military post. These are retained as differently attributed descriptions, with the UN investigation as the main evidence. No household displacement count is given. [UNTSO report S/4270 and reproduced Commission resolutions](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/604465/files/S_SUPP_1960_1--%5EOR_SC_1960_I%5E-EN.pdf); [JTA, 2 February 1960](https://www.jta.org/archive/syrian-military-base-wiped-out-three-israelis-dead-seven-wounded); [Israel's response S/4271, in the UN supplement](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/604465/files/S_SUPP_1960_1--%5EOR_SC_1960_I%5E-EN.pdf).

The same Commission record says a UN observer identified four Mystère jets, a type used by the Israeli Air Force, over Qunaytirah in Syrian territory for about half an hour on 1 February. The Commission condemned this hostile act as an armistice violation. No casualties were reported. [UNTSO report S/4270](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/604465/files/S_SUPP_1960_1--%5EOR_SC_1960_I%5E-EN.pdf).

### Negev Bedouin relocation proposal

Historical scholarship based on archival material reports that, in July, Agriculture Minister Moshe Dayan proposed relocating 500 Bedouin families to Ramle and Jaffa, with IDF and Labor Ministry approval. The source documents planning and individual transfer requests; this review does not establish that the 500-family proposal was implemented or that 500 families were displaced. It is recorded as state population and land policy planning, not an accomplished displacement count. [Nasasra, Indiana University Press, *The Tribal Challenge*, chapter 3](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/3261150d-1153-433f-8175-b91b0ac70629/section/9d25bd58-2385-4694-94aa-56020914d885).

This initial scan found no U.S. citizen or U.S. forces victim lead and no Israeli action in Lebanon; those are search results, not proof of absence. No defensible annual displacement total for 1960 has been identified.

## 1961 initial scan

### Israeli aircraft attack Bedouin livestock in UAR territory (2 March)

The Egyptian-Israel Mixed Armistice Commission found that two Israeli aircraft crossed the international frontier into UAR airspace, flew very low over a Bedouin flock and machine-gunned the grazing animals. It found a few camels and donkeys killed or wounded and condemned Israel for a hostile and inhuman action. The Commission's decision does not report human casualties and does not enumerate the animals. This is a documented Israeli military action in the wider region, outside Palestine proper. [Commission decision S/4770](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/630983/files/S_4770-EN.pdf); [UN Yearbook 1961 summary](https://cdn.un.org/unyearbook/yun/pdf/1961/1961_165.pdf).

### Heavy weapons at the Jerusalem parade rehearsal and April parade (17 March–20 April)

Following a Jordanian complaint and UN military observer investigation, the Jordan-Israel Mixed Armistice Commission found that heavy Israeli armament beyond the agreement's limits was present on Israel's side of the Jerusalem demarcation line during a 17 March parade rehearsal. It named a Centurion tank, Sherman tanks and heavy artillery, found an armistice breach, condemned Israel and called for measures to prevent recurrence. Security Council Resolution 162 endorsed the Commission decision and called on Israel to comply. Israel argued that the display was a peaceful ceremony and cited past parades by Jordan. [Commission decision S/4776](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=S%2F4776&t=pdf); [Security Council meeting S/PV.947](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/631280/files/S_PV-947-EN.pdf); [Resolution 162](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/112156).

JTA reported that the parade proceeded in Jerusalem on 20 April, with tanks and artillery, including U.S.-made recoilless guns mounted on jeeps. That later reporting documents the parade and equipment; the Commission's finding and Resolution 162 concerned the March rehearsal and do not constitute a separate adjudication of the April event. No casualties or displacement were reported in the reviewed accounts. [JTA, 21 April 1961](https://www.jta.org/archive/300-000-spectators-watch-jerusalem-military-parade-air-force-eliminated).

This initial 1961 scan found no U.S. citizen or U.S. forces victim lead and no Israeli action in Lebanon. Those are search results, not proof of absence. No new displacement total is supported by the incidents reviewed.

## 1962 initial scan

### Israeli raid on Syrian positions and villages near Lake Tiberias (16–17 March)

UNTSO documented a sequence of firing incidents before an Israeli military raid on Syrian positions near Lake Tiberias. The Israeli Defence Forces said the operation destroyed Syrian positions after attacks on Israeli boats and fishermen. Syrian complaints described Israeli fire against posts and villages; the UN report records craters and projectile evidence, and a witness account that villagers fled from Nuquieb to El-Koursi. UN observers could not closely follow the night operation and described the investigation as incomplete. The Security Council nevertheless determined that Israel's 16–17 March attack constituted a flagrant violation of Resolution 111 (1956). The Council resolution gives no casualty or displacement total, and UNTSO gives no verified count of villagers who fled. A U.S. State Department memorandum characterized the action as a retaliatory raid by perhaps 300–400 Israeli forces and said Washington opposed retaliatory raids; that is a contemporary U.S. assessment, not an independent victim count. [UNTSO report S/5102](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/627725/files/S_5102-EN.pdf); [Security Council Resolution 171](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/112170); [FRUS, memorandum from Secretary Rusk to President Kennedy](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1961-63v17/d226); [JTA, 19 March 1962](https://www.jta.org/archive/uneasy-quiet-prevails-along-syrian-israeli-border-u-s-watches-situation).

The UNTSO report says observers clearly saw a six-aircraft dogfight in Syrian airspace. Syria alleged that Israeli Mystère jets had violated its airspace, engaged Syrian aircraft and dropped four fuel tanks; a UN observer was shown the tanks and saw a child in hospital with a slight injury. The report does not conclusively establish which aircraft dropped the tanks or the full sequence, so the Israeli attribution remains provisional. [UNTSO report S/5102](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/627725/files/S_5102-EN.pdf).

### Arrests during Beit Netofa pipeline work (August)

JTA reported that Israeli police made night arrests of an unspecified number of Arabs said to oppose excavation for a water pipeline in the Beit Netofa area; work resumed under heavy police guard. The source does not name detainees or give charges, duration, outcomes, or an independent inquiry. Record this as a press-reported arrest episode and state infrastructure conflict, not as proof of unlawful detention or abuse. [JTA, 23 August 1962](https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-resumes-work-on-water-project-inciting-arabs-arrested).

### Two teenage Arab citizens imprisoned after espionage convictions (December)

The dispatch also relayed their statement that Egyptian intelligence had detained and recruited them during a trip to Gaza. This is included as a state capture/prosecution/imprisonment record relevant to the project’s detention scope; the reviewed report alleges no Israeli mistreatment, and no judgment, appeal or prison record has yet been located. [JTA, 28 December 1962](https://www.jta.org/archive/two-israeli-arabs-sentenced-in-beersheba-to-six-years-for-espionage).

UNTSO's report documents Israeli soldiers taking positions while an Israeli bulldozer entered a disputed parcel in the central Israel–Syria demilitarized zone. ### Negev Bedouin building-site and relocation planning (April)

[Indiana University Press, *The Tribal Challenge*](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/the-tribal-challenge-alliances-and-confrontations-in-the-israeli-negev/section/7feeacd4-9719-4505-920d-4e659fa2688d)

### Tel El-Qadi and Israeli air/tank attacks (13 November)

UNTSO observers logged heavy exchanges after a Syrian warning shot was fired at an Israeli patrol. The report says this initial Syrian fire followed an Israeli armored personnel carrier entering about 50 metres into Syrian territory, within a disputed boundary area where Israel was reconstructing a track and drainage ditch. Israeli aircraft then bombed and machine-gunned Syrian positions, and Israeli tanks shelled Tel Azaziat. Observers recorded damage in Nukheila and Abbasieh and bomb evidence near Massada, but could not inspect every reported strike location. The Syrian liaison officer reported seven dead and 26 injured, most civilians; the report also records Israeli military and kibbutz casualties. The Security Council's draft resolution deplored renewed military action and regretted losses on both sides, but was not adopted. [UNTSO report S/6061](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/539917/files/S_6061-EN.pdf); [Security Council debate and vote](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-180865/)

This scan found no verified U.S. citizen or U.S. forces victim lead and no Israeli action in Lebanon for 1964; these are search results, not proof of absence. The total number displaced in 1964 remains unknown.

## 1965 initial scan

### Israeli fire on Syrian cultivators and equipment (13–18 May)

Syria's Security Council letter alleged repeated Israeli fire on Syrian cultivators and agricultural equipment over six days, with crop and livestock damage. The evidence is strongest for 13 May: the Israel-Syria Mixed Armistice Commission chairman's investigation found no evidence of Syrian fire and said Israeli fire targeted equipment and civilians working well inside Syrian territory. He described firing from within the demilitarized zone as a serious breach and prohibited weapons in the Israeli sector as a flagrant violation. The later dates in the complaint remain party allegations in this scan. [Syrian letter S/6382](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/609672/files/S_6382-EN.pdf); [Syrian letter quoting the Commission finding, S/6392](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/609681/files/S_6392-EN.pdf)

### Israeli raid and civilian-home demolition in Jordan (27 May)

The Jordan-Israel Mixed Armistice Commission found Israeli forces crossed the Armistice Demarcation Line, demolished civilian homes, killed two men and three children (the youngest four), and wounded two adults and three children (the youngest two). A Jordanian soldier was also wounded in a separate incident when the force fired on a routine patrol. The Commission found no evidence Jordan was involved in sabotage against Ramat-Hahovesh and condemned the raid as a flagrant violation of the General Armistice Agreement. It did not supply a displaced-person count. [Jordan's transmission of Commission findings, S/6446](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/610114/files/S_6446-EN.pdf)

### Mob attack on Ramleh's Arab quarter (22 August)

After a fatal traffic collision involving an Arab taxi driver and a Jewish youth, hundreds of Jewish Israeli youths attacked Arab residents and attempted to enter Ramleh's Arab quarter. JTA reported at least 12 Arab residents injured and 21 youths arrested; police blocked the attempted entry. A PLO memorandum transmitted by Syria alleged that more than 500 attackers intended to destroy the quarter. The register preserves that claim as attributed advocacy testimony and records the JTA account separately. No displacement was reported. [JTA, 24 August 1965](https://www.jta.org/archive/israeli-police-protect-ramla-arabs-from-attacks-21-youths-arrested); [UN document S/6731](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-212047/)

### Israeli army raid into southern Lebanon (28–29 October)

Lebanon reported that Israeli regular forces entered Houla and Meiss El Jabal, destroyed houses and a spring with three reservoirs, and caused the death of a woman. The Lebanese letter says UN observers investigated on site and photographed the evidence. Israel's reply acknowledged a 'warning action' by an army unit that blew up an empty house and a water installation, and described it as a response to prior sabotage attacks it attributed to armed groups crossing from Lebanon. A U.S. diplomatic record shows the U.S. Assistant Secretary reminded Israel's Foreign Minister of Washington's opposition to unilateral cross-border action. No displaced-person total or final legal ruling was found. [Lebanon's letter S/6898](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/533677/files/S_6898-ES.pdf); [Israel's reply S/6956](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/532109/files/S_6956-EN.pdf); [U.S. FRUS record](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v18/d248)

### State demolition of abandoned Palestinian villages (1965–1969)

Historian Aron Shai's peer-reviewed study traces an Israel Land Administration demolition program, begun in spring 1965, targeting Palestinian villages abandoned during the 1948 war. Because this is a multi-year program and residents had already been displaced, it is recorded as post-displacement destruction and land/landscape policy, not as a count of people newly displaced in 1965. The cited study does not provide a 1965-specific village count. [Shai, *History and Memory* 18, no. 2 (2006)](https://doi.org/10.2979/his.2006.18.2.86)

This scan found no verified U.S. citizen or U.S. forces victim lead for 1965; this is a search result, not proof of absence. The total number displaced in 1965 remains unknown. No annual displacement count is inferred from destroyed homes or from the multi-year village-demolition program.

## 1966 initial scan

### Israeli raids at Rafat and Tel el Arba'in/Jisr Sheikh Hussein (29–30 April)

Two separate Israeli military operations crossed the Jordan-Israel Armistice Demarcation Line. At Rafat, the Jordan-Israel Mixed Armistice Commission, relying on UN military-observer investigation, found 18 houses completely demolished, three slightly damaged, two Jordanian soldiers wounded at the police post, and four horses killed. It said villagers escaped and found no civilian casualties at Rafat. The Commission condemned the attack as a serious and flagrant armistice violation.

In a separate operation at Tel el Arba'in and the Jisr Sheikh Hussein police post, the Commission found 11 civilians killed and three wounded, and approximately ten stone homes badly damaged or demolished. Its decision named the victims and said no evidence indicated that any was armed. Jordan’s initial complaint gave provisional lower casualty numbers and merged reports from the two operations; the final Commission records separate the Rafat findings from the civilian deaths farther east. No measured displacement total is available. [Mixed Armistice Commission decision on Rafat, S/7333](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/527342/files/S_7333-EN.pdf); [Commission decision on Tel el Arba'in, S/7325](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/527138/files/S_7325-EN.pdf); [Jordan's initial complaint, S/7280](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/526088/files/S_7280-EN.pdf)

### Israeli raid on As Samu and surrounding southern Hebron villages (13 November)

UNTSO investigated the Israeli army's large-scale raid into Jordan-administered West Bank territory after a Jordanian complaint. Its military observers saw 125 houses, a clinic, six-classroom school and workshop completely demolished; a mosque and 28 houses damaged; and 20 Jordanian army trucks, two jeeps and one civilian bus demolished. The corrected casualty record is three civilians and 15 Jordanian military personnel killed, and 17 civilians and 37 military personnel wounded. A first-distribution report erroneously listed 97 civilian wounded; the UN issued a corrigendum reducing that number to 17. As Samu was reported to have 5,000 residents, but no source in this scan measured how many actually fled or remained displaced.

The UNTSO report also records the capture of two Jordanian soldiers. A private said he was blindfolded, evacuated, interrogated about military information and handed to Jordanian authorities on 16 November. Major Mohammed Dib Alla died while in Israeli custody despite medical efforts, according to the report. The material documents capture, interrogation and transfer; it does not allege torture in the private's account. Israel said the objective was to demolish empty houses as a warning after a mine killed three Israeli soldiers, and disputed the UN report's scale and sequence. The Security Council deplored the loss of life and property, censured Israel for a large-scale military action in violation of the Charter and General Armistice Agreement, and stated that military reprisals could not be tolerated. In a contemporaneous internal U.S. memorandum, Walt Rostow called the raid disproportionate and aimed at the wrong target; Secretary Rusk later warned that recurrence could prompt review of U.S. military-equipment sales to Israel. [UNTSO report S/7593](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/524059/files/S_7593-EN.pdf); [official UN corrigendum](https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-180234/); [Security Council Resolution 228](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=S%2FRES%2F228%281966%29&t=pdf); [Rostow memorandum to President Johnson](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v18/d333); [Secretary Rusk's warning](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1964-68v18/d343); [JTA contemporary report](https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-retaliates-against-jordan-blows-up-40-jordanian-houses)

## 1972

The initial 1972 scan adds 14 incident, trend and scope records and 18 source records. It draws on UNTSO inquiries, Security Council correspondence and resolutions, the UN Special Committee on Israeli practices, JTA reporting and U.S. press/archival sources. This is a documented lead set, not a comprehensive annual account.

- **U.S. citizens and U.S. forces:** No Israeli-attributed killing or injury to an American citizen or U.S. force member was verified in the sources reviewed for 1972. A Los Angeles pipe bomb damaged the home of Palestinian-American Mohammed Shaath; contemporary U.S. reporting said Shaath was away, and a George H. W. Bush Library finding aid ties the attack to Jewish Defense League members and convictions, with debris narrowly missing his daughter. The Special Committee's A/9801 annex attributes the attack to Israeli agents, but the attribution conflicts with the U.S. reporting and archival account. Because Israeli state involvement is not established, the case is retained as an excluded scope check and is not counted as an Israeli incident.
- **Lebanon, January and February:** Lebanon reported Israeli army incursions into Bint Jbeil and Kfar Haman on 10–11 January, destruction of four houses, and artillery fire at Rachayya al-Fakhar that killed one woman and wounded her husband. For 25 February, Lebanon alleged that 60 Israeli tanks and armored cars crossed under air cover and attacked villages. Israel said it had acted in self-defense against armed-group attacks. Security Council Resolution 313 unanimously demanded that Israel cease ground and air action and withdraw from Lebanon.
- **Lebanon and Syria, June:** Lebanon reported that an Israeli armored unit ambushed a Syrian military delegation and Lebanese escorts inside Lebanon on 21 June. It reported five Lebanese military police killed (four at the scene and one later from wounds), five Syrian officers and one Lebanese officer captured, one Syrian officer wounded and one escape. Israel characterized the personnel it held as prisoners of war and sought a general prisoner exchange. Resolution 316 deplored repeated Israeli attacks on Lebanese territory and population and called for the captured personnel's release; Resolution 317 later deplored their continued detention.
- **Lebanon and Syria, September:** UNTSO investigated the 8 September bombing of Nahr al-Bared camp and reported physical damage; casualty figures conveyed from Lebanese authorities were 3 Lebanese and 7 Palestinians killed, 2 Lebanese and 22 Palestinians wounded. Lebanon's preliminary complaint gave a separate 12 killed/34 wounded across locations and reported 10 killed/2 wounded at Rafid. These figures overlap and should not be added. Syria separately reported one woman killed and several civilians wounded in an Israeli air attack. The United States vetoed a draft Security Council condemnation on 10 September; that vote did not settle the incident evidence. On 16 September, UNTSO partially confirmed an Israeli ground and air assault in southern Lebanon and Israeli forces remaining in Lebanese territory. Lebanon reported 25 civilian deaths, 40 civilian injuries, 18 Lebanese military deaths, 49 military injuries and 5 missing, plus 109 houses destroyed, 324 damaged and two bridges destroyed. Israel said it targeted armed-group bases and denied looting; napalm allegations remain attributed to Lebanon. On 15 October, UN observers confirmed Israeli aircraft bombed localities in southern Lebanon and Bekaa and reported three civilian injuries.
- **Rafah/Sinai displacement and settlement:** The UN Special Committee cited an Israeli press account of an unauthorized transfer of about 6,000 Bedouins from the Rafah salient, destruction of about 24 buildings and water sources, and fencing of roughly 20,000 dunums. Egypt separately alleged more than 10,000 Egyptian citizens were forcibly transferred; Israel rejected the allegations. The UN Yearbook's annual estimate is about 11,000 people displaced by transfers in Gaza and northern Sinai during 1972. The geographic and population categories overlap, so these are not additive and are not established as a Palestinian-only total. A later UN land study reports six new settlements in 1972, five in the Jordan Valley. A/8828 quotes Minister Israel Galili saying no area was out of bounds to Jewish settlement and records settlement measures in Gaza, Sinai and the Golan; plans are kept distinct from completed sites.
- **Deportation, detention and torture documentation:** Jordan reported deportations from the occupied territories, including 14 people in late May/June and 28 across April–June in a separate account; ICRC visits in Amman documented groups of deported people. The totals and overlap are unresolved. UN Special Committee reporting cited 600 administrative detainees in January and lower snapshots later in the year; the counts have different dates and possibly different geographic scope. Its cross-year detention inquiry says the vast majority of 195 witnesses alleged torture or ill-treatment and identifies selected accounts as strong prima facie evidence, while explicitly stating that it had made no definitive judgment beyond doubt in any individual case. This evidence is not a count of 1972 torture incidents.
- **Annual displacement:** About 11,000 is the best located UN annual aggregate for 1972 transfers in Gaza and northern Sinai. It cannot be used as a Palestinian-only figure or added to the Rafah estimates. No comprehensive, deduplicated annual Palestinian displacement flow was identified.
- **Settler attacks:** No independently corroborated incident of violence by Israeli settlers against Palestinians was located in this initial 1972 scan. Settlement establishment and land policy are documented separately above; absence of an identified incident is not evidence that none occurred.

This is an initial scan. Continue with 1973, prioritizing U.S. citizens and forces, Israeli military and settler actions, Palestinian deaths and displacement, detention, land measures and settlement expansion. Keep actions by other actors outside the incident dataset and preserve disputed claims with denials.
