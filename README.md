# Palestine and U.S. Incident Research Compiler

A source-linked research project documenting incidents in Palestine, Lebanon, and closely related regional theaters in which U.S. citizens or U.S. forces were killed, injured, detained, or otherwise harmed by Israeli state actors or pre-state Zionist armed organizations. The initial historical entry is the King David Hotel bombing, 22 July 1946.

## Scope

The primary dataset is limited to harm attributed to Israeli state actors (Israeli military, police, security services, state agencies, and settlers where state protection or involvement is documented) and, in a separate historical category, pre-state armed organizations whose conduct is directly relevant to the formation-era record. Incidents attributed to other actors are excluded from the primary dataset. Out-of-scope comparison events may be noted in a clearly separate section, never merged into counts.

“Americans” and “American forces” are separate populations: U.S. citizens (including dual citizens), U.S. service members, U.S. government personnel, and U.S.-based or U.S.-linked organizations are not interchangeable. U.S. permanent residence or family ties alone do not establish citizenship. Geographic scope covers Palestine and Lebanon, with closely related regional incidents such as the USS *Liberty* attack recorded in a distinct adjacent-theater category.

## Opening case: King David Hotel

The Irgun (Etzel; Hebrew acronym for the National Military Organization) was a pre-state Zionist underground paramilitary organization, associated with Revisionist Zionism and opposed to British rule in Mandatory Palestine. Contemporary British official discourse and later scholarship describe it as a terrorist organization. The bombing occurred in 1946, before Israel was established in 1948; Irgun must not be described as an Israeli state agency at the time. Scholarly description: [Bruce Hoffman, “The bombing of the King David Hotel, July 1946”](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/the-bombing-of-the-king-david-hotel-july-1946/). The U.S. consul’s contemporaneous telegram said Americans in the hotel and vicinity escaped injury; a secondary source describes victim Ezra Sperling as American, but his U.S. citizenship is not established in the sources reviewed. See [case matrix](docs/INCIDENT_MATRIX.md).

## Evidence rules

Every factual assertion must be attributed to a source. Distinguish contemporary primary records, official institutional statements, survivor/family testimony, investigative reporting, scholarship, advocacy reporting, and later summaries. Record contradictions rather than silently resolving them. A source’s political standpoint is metadata for interpretation, not an automatic reason to accept or reject a claim. Do not state a legal conclusion as fact unless a court or other competent body made that finding; label allegations and investigative conclusions by their issuing body and procedural status.

No bulk scraping or automated harvesting is authorized by this scaffold. “Compiler” means a provenance-preserving research workflow at this stage: source discovery, manual extraction, structured records, and verification. Any later automation should respect access restrictions, source safety, copyright, and source provenance.
